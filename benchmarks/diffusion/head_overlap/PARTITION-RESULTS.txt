An even head count alone does not determine FA3 latency. The H3 3K screen
favored odd 7+7 over both even alternatives in all eight paired rounds.
A 128-query-tile, one-resident-CTA-per-SM proxy predicts 8 waves for 7+7
versus 9 for 6+8 or 4+10 at 9248 tokens. All predict 14 at 16416 tokens.
This is a hypothesis, not an observed launch grid or proof of the full-model cause.
Choosing a ratio must balance attention splitting tax and exposed communication edges.
For asymmetric groups, the equal-split half-communication budget is only a reference.
The leading forward and trailing reverse transfer sizes also change with the ratio.
The single-layer gain does not establish the complete-request gain; use confirmations.

Idealized two-group critical path (no fences, local copies or interference)
Let F/R be whole forward/reverse transport, A0/A1 the sequential split attentions,
and x the first group's head fraction. The implemented F0,A0/F1,A1/R0,R1 order gives
T = x*F + max(A0, (1-x)*F) + max(A1, x*R) + (1-x)*R.
Hidden communication = min(A0, (1-x)*F) + min(A1, x*R). Net benefit subtracts
A0+A1-Awhole plus any real copy/fence/interference increase from that hidden time.
Equal groups with enough compute hide half of F+R. Unequal groups change both
the leading/trailing edges and the available compute; half is not a universal invariant.
This model explains the candidate search; it is not a timing measurement.
